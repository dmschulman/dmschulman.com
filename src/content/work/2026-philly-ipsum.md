Like most designers whose ideas coalesce from a concept straight to a wireframe, I turn to a [lorem ipsum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorem_ipsum) generator to simulate webpage content before the real thing is available. This gives me a sense of whitespace and air between sentences as much as it helps reflect what the finish webpage should feel like.

But much to my dismay I learned no lorem ipsum generator existed to express the unique character and historic charm of the great city of Philadelphia (my country). Like Bacon Ipsum and Kitten Ipsum and all the other Ipsums that came before it I decided it would be a fun adventure to spin up my very own Philly Ipsum.

The idea originally began as a practical way for me to apply what I'd been learning as I studied React, but as tends to happen with personal projects walls were encountered, free time diminished, and priorities slowly shifted to other things.

Fast-forward to 2026 and my newfound embrace of [Astro](https://astro.build) and Node I decided to dust the project off and rework the application from the ground up.

The generator uses an extensive series of sentence templates and randomly chosen words from distinct series of wordlists representing people, places, things, phrases, and openers. These elements combine to build somewhat realistic sounding but completely incoherent sentences (which should be the goal of any competent lorem ipsum generator).

Users can select how many paragraphs they need to generate and how many sentences should be contained within each paragraph. Output can be created and regenerated asynchronously making Philly Ipsum a relatively self-contained PWA. You can also choose to wrap HTML tags around each paragraph making it easy to pull the lorem ipsum into a webpage as paragraphs, table cells, or a list (a feature I wish more generators would include).

Visit [PhillyIpsum.com](https://www.phillyipsum.com) today and build a hoagiemouthed fever dream to include in your next website or design project. Go birds!
