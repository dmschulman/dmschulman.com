Not sure if you've seen it yet but I've added a new collection I'm, tentatively, calling the [Media Shelf](/shelf). On the Shelf you'll find a collection of all my favorite records, books, podcasts, mixes, movies, and much much more, because it wasn't enough to write about all my favorites (I want to be able to show you them).

I'd be remiss if I didn't mention [Andy Bell](https://bell.bz/)'s website and his [Music Collection](https://bell.bz/music-collection/) page as seeing that site some time ago definitely planted a seed in my brain. I've wanted to build sometime similar ever since, and although music is the media which I most consume, I wanted to branch out into other areas with my own collection.

The focus of my Shelf is also about sharing these things with you as much as it is about cataloging and curating them. Each item in the collection links out to the thing itself, whether its a book or a movie (which will send you to Amazon) or a podcast, record, or DJ mix. Link rot is a very real thing, so I tried to balance concerns about media disappearing from services as much as I could with using ethical sources for linking through to.

I plan to do a full writeup on how I built the feature, but I wanted to showcase a few of the handy resources I've discovered along the way.

[Ben Dodson's iTunes Artwork Finder](https://bendodson.com/projects/itunes-artwork-finder/) has been indispensable for this project and was the single biggest factor in the digital media shelf transforming from a neat idea to a practical concept. To illustrate each item on the shelf, I start there. iTunes has an almost endless library of album covers, book sleeves, and movie box art to choose from, not to mention it's already hosted online through reliable infrastructure that handles billions of queries every day (probably). Ben's tool has even been handy with covers for some of the DJ mixes I feature.

[last.fm](https://www.last.fm/) has been my alternative source for music and mixes cover art. iTunes has a lot, but last.fm is my go-to for the deep cuts. Both iTunes and last.fm have stood the test of time as well, so I feel like they're as reliable as anything for sourcing images and information for this project. While I haven't kept up my scrobbling as I've moved between machines and OSes over the years, I've [been a member of last.fm since 2007](https://www.last.fm/user/dmschulman)!

[The Internet Archive](https://archive.org/) was a pleasant surprise here, both as a streaming content source and as an album art source. I will definitely be diving further into their collections especially when it comes to DJ mixes and out-of-print books. I can also sleep easy knowing I can safely expand my shelf one day to include my favorite Phish shows and old shareware games.
