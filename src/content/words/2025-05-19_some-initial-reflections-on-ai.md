Like it or not, the whole AI thing is not going away.

While there is *plenty* to talk about right now regarding the development and meteoric rise of LLMs, agentic web, and applications of this new (well, old) technology, I want to keep something in perspective regarding building code in the AI era.

Silicon Valley has already placed their bets when it comes to the future of work with AI. Massive layoffs, driven in large part by over hiring during the pandemic (which was also in turn driven by a near-zero interest rate lending environment), have been plaguing tech workers throughout 2024 and 2025. The idea that super-intelligent AI will replace the need for a large portion of these tech workers has the world's largest companies (and many of the world's smallest startups) salivating over the prospect of increased profits due to decreased head counts.

Silicon Valley is well known for placing bets. The majority of these bets don't work out for most companies. It's honestly the business model of startups, and any large company that likes to style themselves after startup culture, to take a relatively unproven idea and build a company around it. Anxious investors fuel this machine on the hope and a wish of a big payday if the startup works out. Many of these companies *still* don't make a healthy profit relative to the amount of investment they receive. Facebook only accelerated the pace of this culture due to their popular and poorly conceived idea around "move fast and break things".

So it's safe to say that while Microsoft, Google, Facebook, et al love to make moves, the moves they make are reflective of Silicon Valley culture, not the economy. Of course that's not going to stop other businesses (many far away from the tech sector) from trying to emulate those business decisions and institute layoffs in the hopes that AI can replace part of their human workforce. And it of course isn't stopping a lot of big names from doing just that.

And they'll bear the cost of those decisions soon (whatever they may be).

But while AI has come a long way with regards to generating relevant and useful output, it'll only ever be as good as the people helping to build it. AI evangelists will say otherwise, and it's clear that a lot of big (and small) companies are listening to the AI evangelists right now. But this isn't the first and won't be the last bubble big tech has instigated, though you can be assured, AI is truly a bubble that's either on-par or great than the dot-com bubble.

Though as it stands, we're entering the era of AI and all that comes with it. I predict AI will (and has already) created some amazing tools and generated some amazing efficiencies in people's work. But AI is just that, a tool. It doesn't accomplish much by itself, and what it does accomplish with human input also requires a human to check the work and implement it properly.

As much as thought leaders and non-technical founders would want it to be the future, I just don't see the idea of a vibecoding CEO going anywhere (at least in the next 5 years). Though you want to know one thing AI might be really good at? Getting those thought leaders and non-technical founders to start working with programming and helping them hone their development skills. For that, in my opinion, is the actual tangible benefit to this technology right now.

Just be sure to get some humans to check their work every so often...
