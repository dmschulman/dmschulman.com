I think it's worthwhile to capture these trivial thoughts on how I conceptualize the idea of a blog on this website, so I'm going to continue to capture them here.

I consider a blog to be a personal record. The term "blog" was originally used to describe anyone writing in a non-article informal context to a wider audience on the web, which pretty much meant "everyone with a website writing words who wasn't the New York Times". I agree with the informal context part, though many "bloggers" out there (do we still have bloggers?) would consider the act of blogging to be a professional career choice and the words that they've written to be their magnum opus.

And neither of those ideas are wrong! There are plenty of people who've made blogging their full-time, influential, and somewhat lucrative job. I just don't see anything I do on this blog to be that kind of work.

As blogging grew into prominence, writing stuff on your own little piece of the web became (for the majority) less about creating content and more about, well, journaling, to some degree. The ouroboros now finds itself devouring in the other direction, a majority on the web are in it (it now seems) to simply create content and not necessarily share their personal experience or whatever random little idea pops into their head that perhaps more than one person could possibly be interested in. Worth it to note as well, I do think we're on the verge of things moving in the other direction again.

When a blog post rises to the level of "something more" than just rambling, then it becomes a "post", an article with some specific relevance or context. This distinction helps me keep my material organized (I want the really good stuff to stand on its own) but also allows me to get over whatever mental barriers I've put into place around writing for an audience.

In a world where everything is a post, I lose all momentum after getting the initial idea down on paper since I now need to present evidence, do a deeper dive on research, copy edit my words, and all the rest as this dinky little thought is now something I consider an article. If you ever want to stifle your creativity, imagine you're writing something that's going to be published one day.

So then if blogging = small stuff and posts = big stuff, then what happens when a blog post (much like this one) starts to get a little out of hand? I definitely need to reassess my blog index pages as post previews with a "read more" button might become necessary. I've already implemented links to individual blog posts, something I imagined I'd be able to forgo if my blog posts looked more like tweets and less like articles.

And if blogs and posts really are, conceptually, the same thing after all, why keep them separate? Why not just deploy some thoughtful tagging to keep content better organized? In some ways I feel like I'm solving problems Wordpress already handled 20 years ago. But then again, there were some things I never liked about Wordpress in the first place (and even less to like about it now).

In the short term at least, expect the blog index page to look a little different. In the long term? Maybe you'll see some articles in this feed as well.
